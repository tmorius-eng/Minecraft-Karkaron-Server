## 1. Tug / Sülde banners (туг, сүлд)

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A *tug* (*tugh*) is a pole topped with horse-tail (or yak-tail) hair arranged in a circle. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (secondary) | [S20] |
| *Secret History of the Mongols* (§202): in the **Year of the Tiger, 1206**, at the source of the **Onon**, Chinggis raised a **white banner with nine pendants/tails** and became Great Khan. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary text, in translation) | [S21], [S22] |
| "There has never been an exact illustration of the nine-ribbon flag". Modern versions are reconstructions from written descriptions. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (secondary, per a vexillology paper) | [S21] |
| The modern **Nine White Banners** (*Yesön khölt tsagaan tug*): nine poles with white horse hair, a larger central one, and a three-pronged flame finial. They are linked to peacetime/state ceremony. A **black** banner (*khar sülde*) is linked to war and to the spirit of the ruler. | **Partly verified** (secondary only). The exact 13th-century form of the finial and of the black/white split is not shown by sources I could reach. | [S20] |
| *Sülde* (сүлд) means the vital force or guardian spirit tied to a person or the state, carried in the banner. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED as a concept (secondary). Its exact 13th-century meaning is debated. | [S20] |

**SÜLD use.** The server's name and the **Хөх Сүлд (Blue Standard)** relic are **INSPIRED** by the tug/sülde
concept. The Blue Standard's lore, powers and blue colour are **ORIGINAL SULD FICTION**. Historically the banners
are **white** (state) and **black** (war), not blue. Keep the "three-pronged flame" finial out of 13th-century
claims, since only the modern Nine White Banners verifiably use it.

## 2. Soyombo (Соёмбо) — NOT a Mongol Empire symbol

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The Soyombo **script** was created by **Zanabazar in 1686**. The Soyombo **symbol** is a special character of that script. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S23], [S24] |
| The symbol has been a national emblem of Mongolia **since 1911** and is on the flag. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S23] |

**SÜLD rule:** The Soyombo dates from **about 430 years after Rubruck**. It must **never** appear on items, banners or
UI presented as Mongol-Empire-period. If used at all, it belongs to a clearly modern context such as server
branding, and even there it is a national symbol to treat with respect. **Recommendation: do not use it in game art.**

## 3. Imperial seal of Güyük (1246)

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In **1246 Güyük Khan** sent a letter to **Pope Innocent IV** demanding submission. The letter is in the **Vatican Apostolic (formerly Secret) Archives**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S25], [S26] |
| The letter is in **Persian** with a Turkic preamble. Its **seal** is in Mongolian written in the **Uyghur-derived (classical) Mongolian script**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S25] |
| Seal text (one translation): "Under the power of Eternal Heaven, the decree of the Oceanic Khan of the Great Mongol Nation: if it reaches people both subject and rebel, let them revere, let them fear." | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (translation) | [S25], [S27] |

**SÜLD use:** A square seal stamp as a UI motif (quest completion, decree scrolls, admin stamps) is **INSPIRED**.
**Do not reproduce the real seal's text or layout** in a joke or gameplay context. The opening formula "By the
power of Eternal Heaven" is verified and can be quoted with care in lore.

## 4. Paiza / Gerege (пайз, гэрэгэ)

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The **Met** holds a **late-13th-century safe-conduct pass (paiza)**, accession **1993.256**. It is **iron with silver inlay**, about 7⅛ × 4½ in., circular, with an animal-mask top. The openwork inscription is in **ʼPhags-pa script** and reads "By the strength of Eternal Heaven, an edict of the Emperor [Khan]. He who has not respect shall be guilty." | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (museum record) | [S28] |
| The Rubin Museum's Project Himalayan Art has an essay on a Mongol messenger's badge in ʼPhags-pa script. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (exists; content not read in full) | [S29] |

*verified-as-source* base for a SÜLD **rank or authority insignia ladder**. The insignia are INSPIRED. Mapping
them onto the 8 item rarities would be ORIGINAL SULD FICTION.

## 7. Scripts

| Script | Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Uyghur-Mongolian** (*Mongol bichig*, монгол бичиг) | Around **1204**, after defeating the Naiman, Chinggis captured the Uyghur scribe **Tata-tonga**, who adapted the Uyghur alphabet for Mongolian (the "traditional account"). It is written **vertically, columns left to right**, and descends from Sogdian and ultimately Syriac. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (traditional account, widely accepted) | [S37], [S38] |
| **ʼPhags-pa** (дөрвөлжин бичиг, "square script") | Designed by **Drogön Chögyal Phagpa (1235–1280)** for Kublai. A **1269 edict** required it for official documents. It fell out of use after 1368. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S39], [S40] |
| **Soyombo** | 1686, Zanabazar (see §2). | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S23] |

**SÜLD UI implications:**

- Minecraft text renders horizontally. True vertical Mongolian script needs **pre-rendered bitmap glyphs**, for
  example a whole word as one tall glyph in the `suld:ui` font. This is **INSPIRED** decoration only.
- Any Mongolian-script word in SÜLD art must be **checked by a literate reader** before shipping. Mirrored or
  garbled script is a real cultural risk.
- ʼPhags-pa suits **Yuan-era imperial objects** (paiza, seals) after 1269. Uyghur-Mongolian suits the whole
  empire period. Do not mix them up on a "1206" item.
- Player-facing text stays **Cyrillic Mongolian**, as today. Classical script is ornament, never information.
