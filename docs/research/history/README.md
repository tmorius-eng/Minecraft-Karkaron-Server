# SÜLD historical research: Mongol Empire and Kharkhorum

SÜLD is a fantasy MMORPG **informed by** the Mongol Empire and its capital Kharkhorum (Хархорум, Karakorum).
This research separates what the evidence says from what SÜLD adapts or invents, so the game never presents
fiction as history.

## Labels

Every element carries one label.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **HISTORICALLY VERIFIED** | Supported by a cited source. The table states the source's strength: *primary* (a contemporary text in translation, such as Rubruck or the *Secret History*), *museum record*, *peer-reviewed*, *institutional* (UNESCO, DAI, Bonn), or *secondary* (encyclopaedic or popular). |
| **partly verified** / **unverified** | Something is reported, but this session could not confirm it in a reliable source. It must not be presented as fact. |
| **INSPIRED** | A SÜLD design loosely based on a verified element. The table says which one. It may be described as "in the spirit of", never as "this is how it was". |
| **ORIGINAL SULD FICTION** | Invented for the game: powers, lore, mechanics, invented names and places. |

## Method and source-access limits (read this)

Research was done on **2026-10-07** in a sandboxed cloud session. **The network egress policy blocked direct
fetching** of whc.unesco.org, dainst.blog / dainst.org, uni-bonn.de, metmuseum.org, cambridge.org, wikipedia.org,
publicdomainreview.org and the other pages cited. Every fact here was therefore taken from **web-search result
summaries of the cited pages**. Where possible it was cross-checked between two or more independent sources, such
as the DAI and Bonn pages, the Met record and the Rubin essay, or Rubruck and later scholarship.

Consequences:

1. Quotations are as they appeared in search summaries of the cited page or translation. They are **not
   checked character by character** against the original, so re-check before any public lore text quotes them.
2. Numbers that only one secondary source gives are marked as such.
3. Nothing was downloaded into the repository. No images, texts or other third-party assets from these sources
   are stored here, only URLs and our own wording.

**Before public-facing use** (website, lore books, store pages), re-verify each fact against the primary
document. The priority list is in [What still needs verification](#what-still-needs-verification).

### Do NOT use as Mongol-Empire-period elements

| Element | Why |
|---|---|
| Soyombo symbol | 1686 (Zanabazar); a modern national symbol |
| Erdene Zuu architecture, 108-stupa wall | 1585 and later |
| "The 64-column hall was the khan's palace" | Superseded: the Great Hall was a Buddhist temple |
| "Pavilion 90 m / 300 ft tall" | Unverified number |
| A "Borjigin tamga" shape | No securely attested design |
| Blue as the colour of the imperial banner | Banners were white (state) and black (war); blue is SÜLD fiction |
| Long Manchu/Qing-style bows | Later than the empire |
| Tracing ornaments, scroll images or museum photos | Copyright, cultural-respect and accuracy reasons; draw originals |

## What still needs verification

1. UNESCO WHC 1081 page and the ICOMOS evaluation (1081rev.pdf): exact wording and component monuments.
2. Bemmann et al. 2022 (*Antiquity*): wall dimensions, gate positions, the area figures (465 ha vs 4.7 km² surveyed; 1.8 km² city).
3. DAI and Bonn reports: the 1346 inscription's exact name and its link to the Great Hall.
4. The current scholarly view on the palace's location (under Erdene Zuu, or elsewhere).
5. The Met lamellar-helmet accession number.
6. The Rubruck translation (Jackson / Rockhill): the exact wording of the Silver Tree, palace and gate passages.
7. Ger terms (khana, toono, uni) from an ethnographic source.
8. The dating of ulzii and horn ornaments in Mongol-Empire-period objects (museum objects).
