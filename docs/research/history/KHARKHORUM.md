| Date | Event | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

| 1235 | After the defeat of the Jin, **Ögedei** walled the city and built a fixed palace. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S8] |

| 1235–1260 | Karakorum is capital of the Mongol Empire. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S8] |
| 1254 | William of Rubruck, a Franciscan envoy, is at Möngke's court and writes the main eyewitness description of the city. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S10], [S11] |
| 1260 → 1271 | Kublai moves the capital to Shangdu and then Khanbaliq (Dadu). After the Yuan is founded (1271), Karakorum becomes a provincial administrative centre. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S8], [S9] |
| 1346 | An inscription describes building work on the "temple of the rise of the Yuan" in Karakorum (see §4). | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S5], [S6] |
| 1370 | After the Yuan fall (1368), Karakorum becomes the residence of Biligtü Khan (Northern Yuan). | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (secondary) | [S8] |
| 1371/72 | A seal of the Ministry of Finance, made in Karakorum, is dated to these years. It is the most important find of the city-centre excavation. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S4] |
| 1380 | Ming armies sack the city, which effectively ends it as a city. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (secondary) | [S8] |
| 1585/86 | **Erdene Zuu** monastery is founded by **Abtai Sain Khan** on or next to the ruins, partly with stone taken from them. This is **three centuries after the empire's peak**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S12], [S8] |

**SÜLD rule:** Erdene Zuu's 108-stupa wall and its 16th-century Tibetan-Buddhist architecture do **not** belong
in an "imperial Kharkhorum" set in the 13th century. Showing them as the Mongol Empire capital would be an anachronism.

## 3. Shape, walls, gates, roads

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rubruck: apart from the khan's palace, the city "is not as big as the village of Saint Denis". He says the monastery of Saint-Denis is ten times larger than the palace. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary, in translation) | [S10] |
| Rubruck: the town is enclosed by a **mud wall** and has **four gates**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary) | [S10] |
| Rubruck's gate markets: **east** millet and grain; **west** sheep and goats; **south** cattle and wagons; **north** horses. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary) | [S10] |
| The 2016–2017 geophysical survey (SQUID magnetometry, about 4.7 km² in and around the city) mapped the walls and **three of the four** historically recorded gates. It found that the city extended **more than 3 km along the approach roads** outside the walls, and that about **40 % of the walled area was empty**. Neighbourhoods can be told apart by building type and use of space. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (peer-reviewed) | [S2], [S3] |
| Under Ögedei the city is reported to have covered about **1.8 km²**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (secondary; figure not checked against the Antiquity paper) | [S6] |
| Two main axes ran east–west and north–south and crossed in the centre. The Bonn excavation lay "on the elevated area south of the central intersection of the two main axes". | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S4] |
| Wall lengths (about 1,500 m N/S sides, 580–1,120 m E/W sides, about 5.2 km perimeter) come from the existing brief. **They were not re-checked in this session.** | unverified here (see brief) | KHARKHORUM_RESEARCH_BRIEF.md |

Interpretation, not fact: the empty 40 % and the extramural ribbon development fit a city where many people
lived in gers and only craftspeople and labourers lived there permanently. The survey reporting offers this
as a possibility, not as a conclusion [S3].

## 4. Palace, or Buddhist temple? The "Great Hall" debate

This is the most important correction for SÜLD's palace design.

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In the 1940s, Kiselev excavated a large columned hall and **identified it as Ögedei's palace**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S5], [S6] |
| The Mongolian-German Karakorum Expedition (MDKE) has excavated since 1999/2000. Partners: the Institute of Archaeology of the Mongolian Academy of Sciences (D. Bayar, U. Erdenebat), the DAI's Commission for Archaeology of Non-European Cultures (H.-G. Hüttel, C. Franken) and the University of Bonn (H. Roth, E. Pohl). | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S5], [S4] |
| The MDKE results led to the conclusion that the hall was a **large Buddhist temple of the 13th–14th centuries**, not the palace. DAI publications moved from calling it the "Palast" to the more neutral "Große Halle" (Great Hall). | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S5], [S6] |
| Hüttel's own 2009 paper is titled roughly "Royal palace or Buddhist temple? On the search for the Karakorum palace". This shows that the identification was openly debated and changed. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (title) | [S6] |
| The Great Hall had an **8 × 8 grid of columns**. At its centre stood a large **stupa** with at least four larger-than-life Buddha figures, and the stupa was filled with thousands of *tsatsa* votive clay offerings. The **building technique is Chinese; the plan follows a Tibetan mandala**. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S5], [S6] |
| A **1346 inscription** describes building the "temple of the rise of the Yuan" (often given as *Xingyuan ge*). The hall is linked to it. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (the link to the hall is the excavators' interpretation) | [S6] |
| A claim that this pavilion was "300 feet (90 m) tall" appears in encyclopaedic summaries. | **unverified, treat with suspicion** | [S8] |
| **Where was the real palace?** Work on the grounds of Erdene Zuu exposed parts of a large gateway into a "palace city". A chronicle and re-used inscriptions in the monastery walls support the idea that the monastery stands on Ögedei's residence. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED that the hypothesis exists; the palace's exact location is **not settled** | [S7], [S8] |

**What SÜLD may and may not say:**

- OK: "The khans' palace, the *Tumen Amgalan*, was built in 1235–36. Its exact remains are still debated by archaeologists."
- NOT OK: "The 64-column hall was Ögedei's throne room." The 8 × 8 column grid is **verified, but for the Buddhist
  Great Hall**. The existing brief's "throne hall uses an 8 × 8 grid of wooden columns" is therefore an
  **INSPIRED** design choice. It borrows the temple's grid for a palace and must not be described as the palace's real plan.
- Rubruck describes Möngke's audience hall as "like a church, with a middle nave, and two sides beyond two rows of
  pillars", with the khan raised on a dais at one end [S10], [S11]. This basilica-like plan with a raised throne is
  the verified basis for a SÜLD throne hall.

## 5. The Silver Tree (Мөнгөн мод)

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rubruck met the Parisian goldsmith **Guillaume Boucher** (William of Paris) at Karakorum in 1254. Boucher made a **silver tree** fountain for Möngke. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary) | [S10], [S13] |
| At the tree's roots were **four silver lions**, each with a pipe pouring **white mare's milk**. Four pipes ran up inside the tree to its top and then down. Each was shaped as a **gilded serpent** whose tail wound round the trunk. They poured **wine, clarified mare's milk (*caracosmos*), *bal* (a honey drink) and rice mead**, each into its own silver basin at the foot. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary) | [S10], [S13] |
| At the top was an **angel holding a trumpet**. A man hidden in a vault under the tree blew through a pipe when signalled; the angel "sounded", and servants then poured the drinks into the pipes. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary) | [S10], [S13] |
| No physical remains of the tree are known. All reconstructions, including the modern monument in Ulaanbaatar, are interpretations. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (absence of evidence, per sources consulted) | [S13] |

SÜLD use: a Silver Tree in the palace courtyard is **INSPIRED**, and that is defensible. Any gameplay effect of
the tree (buffs, quests) is **ORIGINAL SULD FICTION**.

## 6. Quarters, crafts and trade

| Claim | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rubruck names two quarters: the **Saracens'** (Muslim merchants), with the markets, and the **Cathayans'** (northern Chinese), "who are all artisans". | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (primary) | [S10], [S14] |
| Bonn's excavation in the city centre found **workshops**: **metalworking**, **glass and gemstone working**, **bone carving** and **birch-bark objects**, each shown by raw material, half-finished pieces, waste and finished goods. The excavators assign the quarter to the "Cathai" and place it on the main street. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S4] |
| East of the main street was a metal craftsman's workshop from the first half of the 13th century. Anvils set in round wooden stakes were used for **bronze** working in front of the workshop. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S4] |
| Kilns south-west of the Great Hall fired **roof tiles, wall bricks and architectural terracottas shaped like dragon heads** for the temple roof. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED | [S6], [S15] |
| Glazed tile fragments: a dragon-head ornament with mottled **green** glaze and **yellow** details is described in a museum record. **Yellow and red** glazed tile is also reported. | HISTORICALLY VERIFIED (museum records; the "imperial-only colours" reading in [S15] is that museum's interpretation) | [S15], [S16] |

SÜLD implications. The brief's "Craft Quarter around the central crossroads" is VERIFIED in spirit. **Bronze,
green-glazed roof tile, birch bark, bone and glass** are verified material vocabulary for the city's buildings
and props. "Grey European castle stone" is not.

## 9. Corrections to `docs/world/KHARKHORUM_RESEARCH_BRIEF.md`

Corrections for the world team to apply. **This file does not edit the brief.**

1. **"Palace in the south-west … the excavated Great Hall"**: The Great Hall is now interpreted as a **Buddhist temple**.
   The palace's location is unresolved; one hypothesis puts it under Erdene Zuu. A SÜLD palace in the south-west
   is a fine design choice but should be labelled INSPIRED.
2. **"64 wooden columns on granite bases" for Ögedei's palace**: The 8 × 8 = 64 column grid is verified for the
   **Great Hall / temple**. Keep the design, but re-label it as INSPIRED by the Great Hall.
3. **"Four turtles mark the gates"**: tourist tradition; INSPIRED.
4. **Wall dimensions**: not re-verified in this session.
