# Kharkhorum — Historically Informed City-Planning Brief

SÜLD's spawn is an original fantasy city, **informed by** the real Mongol capital Karakorum (Хархорум),
not a reconstruction of it. This brief separates **what the evidence says** from **what SÜLD adapts or
invents**, so we never present fiction as history.

Research method (2026-10-06): this cloud sandbox could not open unesco.org, medievalists.net or
orchontal.de directly (network policy). The facts below were collected through web search over the
sources listed at the end, cross-checked between independent sources where possible, and combined with
the UNESCO facts the project owner supplied. **Before any of this is used in public-facing lore, re-check
every fact against the primary documents.**

## 1. What the evidence says

### Foundation and status
- The Orkhon Valley World Heritage listing describes Kharkhorum as the capital founded by **Chinggis Khan
  in 1220** (UNESCO, per the project owner's reference).
- In **1235 Ögedei Khan** surrounded the city with walls and built a palace that was **supported by 64
  wooden columns on granite bases**. Contemporary descriptions add gilded columns, pavilions, gold and
  silver basins, a wine cellar, and wall paintings by Khitan artists (secondary summaries of the
  historical record).

### Shape and size
- The outer city was an **irregular rectangle**. The north and south walls were about **1,500 m** long.
  The east and west walls were about **1,120 m at their longest and 580 m at their shortest**, giving a
  perimeter of about **5.2 km**.
- The wall was a **mud (earthen) wall** (William of Rubruck, 1254).
- Settlement extended **beyond the walls**. The 2021–22 geophysical survey mapped the city's extent outside
  the walled area.

### Gates, streets and markets
- **Four gates, one in each side.** **Two main streets, east–west and north–south**, met at the centre and
  led to the gates. The 2021–22 survey located three of the four historically recorded gates.
- **A market at each gate** (Rubruck): *"At the east gate are sold millet and other kinds of grain…; at the
  western, sheep and goats…; at the southern, cattle and wagons; and at the northern, horses."*

### Quarters and crafts
- Rubruck names **two quarters**: one of the **Saracens** (Muslim merchants), where the markets were and
  where many people gathered because of the court, and one of the **Cathayans** (northern Chinese), who
  were all **artisans**.
- **The central crossroads was the artisans' quarter.** Excavations there in the early 2000s found
  deposits up to **4 m deep**: housing mixed with intense workshops for **blacksmithing, silver- and
  goldsmithing, glass, and stone working**.

### Palace and compounds
- **The palace stood in the south-west of the outer city**, walled, with four gates. Its walls formed an
  irregular square of about **255 m × 220–255 m**.
- **A cordon of walled compounds of varying sizes** lay between the palace area and the densely built
  middle of the city.
- The excavated "Great Hall" in the south-west, long identified as the palace, is associated with the area
  where the **Erdene Zuu monastery** (late 16th century) now stands. Its identification has been debated
  in the research literature, so treat "this was the palace hall" as uncertain.

### Faiths
- Rubruck counted **twelve temples ("idol temples") of different peoples, two mosques, and one Christian
  church "at the extreme end of the city."** Karakorum was strikingly cosmopolitan.

### Famous details (well documented)
- **The Silver Tree**: a drinks fountain in the shape of a silver tree, made for Möngke Khan by the Parisian
  goldsmith Guillaume Boucher. Rubruck describes it.
- **Stone turtles**: granite turtle sculptures (stele bases) are associated with the site; one is a famous
  surviving monument near Erdene Zuu.

## 2. What this means for the SÜLD city plan

| Historical evidence | SÜLD design decision |
|---|---|
| Irregular rectangle, wider east–west (≈1500 m) than north–south (≈1120 m), one shorter side | City footprint **352 × 272 blocks** (x −176…175, z −136…135). The north-east corner is cut so the east wall is shorter, an *irregular* outline rather than a castle square. About 1:4–1:6 scale: big enough to feel imperial, small enough to cross in about a minute. |
| 4 gates, one per side; 2 main streets crossing at the centre | **Four gates** (N, E, S, W) joined by a 7-wide **N–S avenue** and **E–W street**. **Spawn plaza at the crossroads.** |
| Gate markets: E grain, W sheep/goats, S cattle/wagons, N horses | **Grain Gate market** (east), **Sheep Gate pens** (west), **Wagon & Cattle Yard** (south), **Horse Gate market + stables** (north). |
| Artisans' quarter at the central crossroads | The **Craft Quarter** wraps the spawn plaza, so the **blacksmith is minutes from spawn**, matching the first-time player flow. |
| Saracen merchants' quarter with the markets | The **Great Market** lies along the E–W street toward the Grain Gate: caravans, cloth, spices, weapons. |
| Palace walled, south-west, four gates, ~255 m square | **Imperial Palace** in the **south-west**, its own wall and four gates, about 97 × 93 blocks, on a raised terrace. |
| 64 wooden columns on granite bases | The **throne hall uses an 8 × 8 grid of wooden columns on stone plinths**: a historically grounded signature detail. |
| Cordon of walled compounds between palace and centre | The **Clan District** is a belt of **walled compounds** between the palace and the craft core. Clan halls are compounds, not castles. |
| 12 temples, 2 mosques, 1 church | A **Temple Quarter** of many small sacred buildings (north-west). See the respect rule below. |
| Settlement beyond the walls | **Outer camps** (yurt suburbs) outside the walls, planned for a later expansion. |
| Silver Tree fountain; stone turtles | **Silver Tree fountain** in the palace courtyard. **Stone turtles** mark the four gate markets. |
| Mud wall | Walls use **packed mud / earth tones** (mud bricks, packed mud, terracotta, timber), *not* grey European castle stone. |

## 3. SÜLD inventions (fantasy, clearly not history)
- **Сүлдэн Багана (the Standard Pillar)**, the central monument of the spawn plaza. Its sky-and-wolf-and-eagle
  symbolism is **SÜLD's own fiction**.
- The **Tenger Sky Shrine**, **Eagle Monument**, **Wolf Monument** and **Horse Monument**: original SÜLD
  landmarks.
- The **Relic Hall** (world-unique relics), the **Hidden Undercity** (old cellars and kiln tunnels; it
  *borrows* the idea of the 4 m-deep craft deposits but is fiction), and the **water channel** and garden
  stream (no evidence of canals; added for gameplay and landscape).
- **Dark-fantasy tone**: weathered timber, soot, ominous banners and night lighting are art direction, not
  claims about the 13th century.

**Respect rule.** Real faiths appear only as respectful architecture: quiet, historically inspired buildings
with no combat, desecration or mockery mechanics. SÜLD's invented spirituality lives in the Tenger Sky
Shrine.
